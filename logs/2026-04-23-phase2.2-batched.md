# 2026-04-23 — Phase 2.2, batched entry point

**Status:** landed
**Goal:** amortize the per-call PyO3 / allocation overhead across many
tiles by accepting 3-D inputs and looping inside Rust.

## What changed

- [fastmma_rust/src/lib.rs](../fastmma_rust/src/lib.rs): added `mma_f32_out_batched` taking
  `(B, M, K) / (B, K, N) / (B, M, N)` and producing `(B, M, N)`. Inner
  loop dispatches into the existing `run_one_tile` helper (refactored
  out so both entry points share kernel code).
- [fastmma/rust_ref.py](../fastmma/rust_ref.py): `mma.call_batched(A, B, C)` — same arg shapes,
  returns a stacked torch tensor. TF32 mask and contiguity handled the
  same way as the single path.
- [bench/batched.py](../bench/batched.py): microbench. Stacks 1024 corpus tiles into one call,
  reports per-tile cost vs the single-shot path, and verifies every
  batched output is bit-identical to its corpus fixture.

## Interpretation

Amortization saves **5–7 µs per tile** — consistent with the earlier
estimate that the PyO3 boundary + output-Vec allocation was ~7 µs fixed.
After amortization, per-tile cost is **pure kernel work**.

The interesting observation: **per-tile cost scales linearly with k**,
not with the `m × n` output footprint. Batched us/tile:
- k=4 (volta):      2.34
- k=8 (turing):     5.08
- k=16 split (amp): 10.45

That's ≈ 0.6 µs per k per tile. With 128 outputs per tile and k terms per
output, that's ≈ 5 ns per (output, term) pair — roughly 15 cycles at 3 GHz.
Each pair does: two i64 multiplies (product sig, aligned add), one int
compare (max_e), one signed division (align), one i64 add. ~15 cycles
looks right for that much work; not much headroom on scalar.

**Where the remaining 10⁶× gap has to come from:**

1. **SIMD on the inner loop.** The outer (M, N) loop is completely
   independent per output. AVX-512 gives 8 i64 adds / 4 i64 muls per
   instruction. NEON gives 2. On this Apple Silicon box, SVE/NEON
   should give ~2–4× for free, and on a server AVX-512 machine ~4–8×.
2. **Multi-threading across tiles.** For `batch ≫ 1`, rayon can split
   the outer loop across cores. Another ~4–8× on a typical machine.
3. **Reduce per-output work.** The current inner loop has a lot of
   conditional logic (subnormal flush check, trunc-to-zero division).
   Specialized kernels per (dtype, nfb, split_k) combo could cut
   constant factors by 2–3× by eliding dead branches and unrolling.

Combined ceiling: 2–4× SIMD × 4–8× threads × 2–3× specialization ≈
**50–100× more** on top of current batched, putting the workhorse in
the 10⁴–10⁵× range per tile. Still short of the nominal 10⁶× target, but
getting closer. The last factor probably requires GPU or accepting that
10⁶× is only reachable in full-GEMM mode (many MMAs × parallel cores).

## Notes

- Corpus only has 16 samples/instruction; the batched bench cycles
  through them to reach 1024 tiles. Correctness check covers the first
  16 (beyond that we're re-running identical inputs).
- `MAX_ELEMS = 256` on stack buffers — fine for the phase-2 set, will
  need to bump when we add block-scaled mxfp4 (k=64 → m·k=1024).
- No multi-threading yet: the inner loop in `mma_f32_out_batched` is
  strictly sequential over the batch.

## What's next

Phase 2.3 — SIMD on the inner loop. Target the (M, N) reduction with
NEON intrinsics via `std::simd` or `core::arch::aarch64`. Expected: 2–4×
per-tile on Apple Silicon. Same corpus gate.

Alternatively, **if the ultimate goal is full-GEMM simulation** (many
tiles in a realistic workload), a rayon-parallel batched entry would
already cash in ~4–8× on a typical laptop and ~10× on a server. That's
faster to deliver and probably more valuable than SIMD at this stage.
Worth discussing before picking.
